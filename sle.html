Know your SLE

What is SLE.

The French proficiency test for Canada is referred to as the Second Language Evaluation (SLE). This is a test used to assess a public service job candidate or employee’s proficiency in their second official language (English or French). The SLE consists of three parts: reading comprehension, written expression, and oral proficiency, in that order.

SLE tests are graded with three proficiency levels: Level A, Level B, and Level C. In this system, Level C is the most advanced level. The level obtained in writing, reading, and oral evaluations are used to create a candidate’s profile, such as BBB or CBC

Route B

Prepares Level B

BBB

You have Level A · Now targeting Level B

BBB is the standard requirement for most bilingual officer-level positions. Level B means you can function, not just survive, in French at work.

After Route B, you will be able to

  • ✓Grasp the main idea of most work-related texts
  • ✓Write with correct grammar on work-related topics
  • ✓Participate in bilingual meetings and express opinions
  • ✓Describe processes and handle workplace conversations

BBB is required for most bilingual federal positions under the PSEA.

Route C

Prepares Level C

CBC / CCC

You have Level B · Now targeting Level C

CBC opens management. CCC opens leadership. Level C is where French becomes a career accelerator.

After Route C, you will be able to

  • ✓Understand complex texts, inferences, and fine points of meaning
  • ✓Write formal explanations in varied work contexts
  • ✓Handle hypotheticals, recommendations, and abstract reasoning
  • ✓Maintain a confident, engaged tone under evaluator pressure

The gap between B and C is not vocabulary. It is strategy: knowing what the evaluator needs to hear and delivering it calmly.
